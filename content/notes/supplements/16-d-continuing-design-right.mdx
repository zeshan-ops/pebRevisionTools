## The problem Brexit created

Before IP completion day, an **unregistered Community design** (UCD) protected a
design across the EU, including the UK, for **three years from first disclosure
in the EU**. When the transition period ended those rights would have stopped
covering the UK, cutting short protection people already held.

Two rights were created to deal with it — one looking backwards, one forwards.

## Continuing unregistered design — the backward-looking fix

Under SI 2019/638 reg 4, a design **protected as a UCD immediately before IP
completion day (31 December 2020)** continues to be protected in the UK. That
design "is referred to as a **continuing unregistered Community design**".

Two points carry the marks:

- It is a **continuation**, not a new right. It does not restart a term.
- It runs for the **remainder of the original three-year UCD term**, measured
  from the design's first disclosure in the EU — not three years from IP
  completion day.

## Why they are now extinct

This is what outcome (d) is really asking. Work it through:

- A CUD had to already be a subsisting UCD on **31 December 2020**.
- A UCD lasts three years from first disclosure.
- So the **longest-lived** possible CUD is one first disclosed on 31 December
  2020, expiring three years later.

**The last continuing unregistered designs expired on 31 December 2023.** No CUD
subsists now; the category is spent and matters only historically, or for acts
done while one was still in force.

## Contrast with supplementary unregistered design

The forward-looking counterpart. **SUD** arises for designs **first disclosed in
the UK on or after 1 January 2021**, and lasts **three years** from that
disclosure. It has broadly the same scope as the UCD it replaces.

| | CUD | SUD |
|---|---|---|
| Applies to | UCDs subsisting on 31 Dec 2020 | Designs first disclosed in UK from 1 Jan 2021 |
| Term | Remainder of the original 3-year UCD term | 3 years from first disclosure |
| Status now | **Extinct** — all expired by 31 Dec 2023 | Live |

**Watch the disclosure trap.** First disclosure in the EU can destroy the
novelty needed for a UK SUD, and first disclosure in the UK can destroy it for
an EU UCD. Simultaneous disclosure is not straightforwardly a solution, and a
client planning a launch should be advised which market matters more.
