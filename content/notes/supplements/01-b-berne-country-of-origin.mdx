## Why country of origin matters

Berne works on **national treatment** (art 5(1)): an author enjoys in each
Union country the rights that country grants its own nationals. Country of
origin matters because several Berne rules turn on it — most importantly the
**comparison of terms** in art 7(8), where a country need not grant a longer
term than the country of origin does.

## The art 5(4) test

Work through the limbs in order; the first that applies gives the answer.

**(a) Works first published in a Union country** — that country.

  - Published **simultaneously in several Union countries granting different
    terms** → the country whose legislation grants the **shortest term**.

**(b) Published simultaneously in a non-Union country and a Union country** →
the **Union country**.

**(c) Unpublished works, or works first published outside the Union without
simultaneous publication in a Union country** → the Union country of which the
**author is a national**, subject to two overrides:

  - **(i) Cinematographic works** whose maker has their headquarters or habitual
    residence in a Union country → that country.
  - **(ii) Works of architecture erected in a Union country**, or other artistic
    works incorporated in a building or structure located in a Union country →
    that country.

## Two definitions the test depends on

**Published** (art 3(3)) requires copies to have been made available in
sufficient quantity to satisfy the reasonable requirements of the public.
Performance, broadcasting, exhibition and public recitation are **not**
publication.

**Simultaneous publication** (art 3(4)) means published in two or more countries
**within thirty days** of first publication. Limbs (a) and (b) both turn on it,
so state the thirty-day rule when applying them.

## Exam technique

The 2022 mark scheme awarded marks limb by limb, including the provisos. It is a
recall question: work through (a), (b), (c) in order and do not stop at the limb
that happens to fit the facts — the marks are spread across the structure.

Note the deliberate trap in limb (a): different terms trigger the *shortest
term* rule, not the longest.
